Method in short. Cases: random entries of a list of 13,115 PDB entries (X-ray, resolution 1.8 A or better, R-free 0.25 or lower, one per 30% sequence-identity cluster; files up to 450 kB, 1 to 8 chains), 150 released from 2024-01-01 and 150 before; the structures used while the thresholds were set are excluded. MolProbity ran on 293 of the 300 (it failed on 7). Clashes are compared by residue pair with MolProbity's severe clashes (overlap 0.4 A or more after Reduce adds hydrogens), Ramachandran and rotamer outliers by residue. For the recent entries the Ramachandran and rotamer references were built from entries released before 2024 only, so these two comparisons are independent of the references.

The other way round, and per structure

ifacekit's 628 unfavorable residue pairs: 497 (79.1%) are in MolProbity's severe list, 72.1% in its heavy-atom list. Its 12,563 borderline pairs agree in 14.6%: borderline is a "look at it" category and not a clash list. Of the 5,466 MolProbity clashes that involve a hydrogen, the residue pair is flagged in 35.4%. Per structure, ifacekit's unfavorable pairs per 1000 atoms follow MolProbity's clashscore with a Spearman correlation of 0.56, and the count of unfavorable pairs follows MolProbity's heavy-atom clash count with 0.76; in 112 of 293 structures ifacekit has no unfavorable pair although MolProbity reports severe clashes in all but 5. ifacekit is not a clashscore.

Clashes with hydrogens added

With the option --hydrogens (or the check box of the portal) ifacekit places hydrogens on the protein itself, does not use Reduce, and reports all-atom clashes: an overlap of 0.4 A or more between atoms more than four bonds apart (the radii of the Probe program), a polar hydrogen next to an acceptor counting as a hydrogen bond. The hydrogen positions of the non-rotating groups agree with Reduce's (97% within 0.15 A on three structures, and 96% of the Asn and Gln flips on 40 cases, see the design document, section 5.13); methyl groups are staggered, hydroxyl, thiol and NH3 groups are turned, and Asn, Gln and His are flipped and protonated to the state with the best contacts. Clashes are compared by residue pair (two atoms of the same two residues are one pair) with MolProbity's severe clashes, those between heavy atoms and those that involve a hydrogen. The settings (hydrogen-bond overlap limits, the bond count that excludes close atoms, the gain a flip must show) were fitted on the first 40 cases; the other 253 cases were not used for any setting.

All 293 cases: ifacekit finds 3,779 of the 4,781 clash pairs of MolProbity = 79.0%. Of the 4,914 pairs ifacekit reports, 3,779 (76.9%) are also in MolProbity's list.

The 253 cases that were not used for tuning: ifacekit finds 3,333 of the 4,222 clash pairs of MolProbity = 78.9%. Of the 4,336 pairs ifacekit reports, 3,333 (76.9%) are also in MolProbity's list.

Why the two lists differ

3,779 pairs are in both lists. 1,002 pairs (21% of MolProbity's) are only in MolProbity's list and 1,135 (23% of ifacekit's) only in ifacekit's. Each pair was given the most likely cause by rules (tools/explain_hydrogen_disagreement.py); the causes are attributions, not proofs. Both programs make choices: MolProbity places hydrogens with Reduce and finds overlaps with Probe's dots, ifacekit places the hydrogens itself and measures overlap between atom centres. The two lists were never going to be identical.

Not found: clashes of hydrogens that ifacekit does not place (ligands, waters, nucleic acids, modified residues), clashes between atoms of one residue and its neighbour that MolProbity counts but the bond-count rule excludes, and groups where Reduce makes a different choice of flip, protonation or rotation. Hydrogens added by ChimeraX instead

A check with another source of hydrogens, not part of ifacekit: ChimeraX 1.12 (2026-06-12) added hydrogens (addh hbond true, which also protonates waters and ligands) to the same 293 models (first conformer, hydrogens and other conformers removed first), and the clashes were found in three ways and compared with the same 4,781 MolProbity pairs. ChimeraX placed 1,007,453 hydrogens, ifacekit 795,193. ChimeraX puts C-H at 1.09 A (the nuclear distance), while the radii of the clash rules belong to the shortened X-ray distances that MolProbity uses; the second line moves the ChimeraX hydrogens to those distances. The last line is the clash finder of ChimeraX itself (overlap 0.4 A, hydrogen-bond allowance 0.6 A). ChimeraX does not flip Asn, Gln and His.

What this comparison does not show

  • All structures are high-resolution X-ray entries of the kind that was used to build the references. Low-resolution models, cryo-EM models, AI predictions and nucleic acids were not tested, and the differences may be larger there.
  • MolProbity's clashes that involve hydrogens (76% of all of them) are not evaluated by the heavy-atom tables of the reports; the option --hydrogens evaluates them (section "Clashes with hydrogens added"), except for ligands, waters and nucleic acids, which get no hydrogens.
  • Clashes inside one residue and between neighbouring residues are only partly found, by design.
  • The rotamer cut-offs were calibrated against MolProbity; Asn is still over-called on recent entries; Cys, His, Pro and Trp keep uncalibrated cut-offs (too few outliers). Ramachandran, interface areas, bonds and the flip check use the first conformer.
  • MolProbity failed on 7 cases (9SQK, 9GIR, 5BMT, 5NAK, 5LOM, 4L9B, 5KO4): Phenix stopped on TLS selections written in braces in the file header. They are listed below.
